## 2. Channel & format read

**Genre:** whiteboard / "doodly" marker-explainer in the personal-finance and money-mindset niche.
This is one of the most templated formats on Shorts precisely because it is cheap to produce and
retains well — the drawing itself is the retention device.

**Production signature (consistent across all three scenes):**

- **Camera:** static, locked overhead close-up. Never moves. No cuts — scene changes are just new
  drawings entering frame.
- **Surface:** stark white background, high-key even lighting, soft shadow under the hand.
- **Talent:** a single right hand with a silver-bodied black-tip marker. No face, no set, no location.
- **Palette:** bright primary/secondary colors on white — green for money, gold for coins, red
  reserved for emphasis words, blue for the "other" party in a comparison.
- **Pace:** roughly **3–4 seconds per scene**, one idea per scene, no dead air.
- **Audio:** calm, plain-spoken male-register voiceover. Conversational, not shouty. No music cue
  detected in the analysis.

**Scene grammar — the reusable unit:** every beat is `one visual metaphor + one sentence`. The
drawing lands at the same moment the sentence lands. Nothing is drawn that the voiceover doesn't
name, and nothing is said that isn't drawn.

## 3. Hook anatomy — why the first four seconds work

The opening is doing four jobs at once, and that's what's worth copying:

1. **Question form.** "Have you heard the phrase…?" opens a loop. A viewer can't answer a direct
   question without staying to hear where it goes.
2. **Borrowed familiarity.** It leads with a saying the audience already knows. Recognition feels
   like agreement, and agreement in second two is what buys second five.
3. **Literal listening cue.** The character cups a hand behind his ear under a megaphone — the
   visual *is* the instruction "listen." The image tells you what to do with your attention before
   the argument starts.
4. **Deliberate withholding.** The hook states the cliché but not the mechanism. The payoff isn't
   information, it's *validation* — "it's not just a phrase… it's the truth." That's the actual
   contract: the video promises to prove something the viewer already suspects.

**The retention engine:** an unfinished drawing is an open loop. The hand is always mid-stroke, so
there's always a shape resolving. That's why the format survives without cuts, music stings, or
a face — the marker does the work a jump cut normally does.

**Structural template extracted:**

```
HOOK        (0-4s)   familiar phrase, posed as a question + a "listen" visual
TURN        (4-7s)   "it's not just X" — escalate the stakes, show the mechanism as a diagram
ASSERTION   (7-10s)  name it in one bold red word
→ MECHANISM (10-35s) explain WHY, one metaphor per beat
→ REFRAME   (35-45s) flip it from doom to something the viewer controls
→ CTA       (45-50s) soft, curiosity-based
```

The reference clip's analyzed portion covers the first three rows. The script below builds out the
whole arc on the same skeleton.

## 4. Original script — "Why Your Savings Account Is Quietly Shrinking"

Same format, same pacing, different topic. Runtime ≈ 48 seconds. Every scene is
`one drawing + one line`. Bold red is reserved for exactly three words across the whole video.

---

**SCENE 1 — 0:00–0:04 · HOOK**
**VO:** "Have you ever been told that saving money is the safe option?"
**DRAW:** Cartoon woman, light complexion, dark hair, mustard-yellow sweater, sitting on a fat green
piggy bank with both arms wrapped around it protectively. A small teal shield icon floats beside her.
Static overhead, white background.

**SCENE 2 — 0:04–0:08 · TURN**
**VO:** "It isn't. It's just a slower way to lose."
**DRAW:** The shield cracks — a jagged black line splits it down the middle. Draw a small downward
red arrow next to the piggy bank.

**SCENE 3 — 0:08–0:12 · ASSERTION**
**VO:** "Because your money has a leak."
**DRAW:** Flip to a glass jar with a green dollar sign, filled with green bills. Add three small
teal droplets falling from a crack near the jar's base, pooling below. Write **"LEAK"** in bold red
above the jar.

**SCENE 4 — 0:12–0:16 · NAME THE MECHANISM**
**VO:** "It's called inflation. Every year, prices go up — your dollars stay the same."
**DRAW:** Split the frame with a horizontal black divider. Top: a shopping cart with a black upward
arrow climbing beside it. Bottom: a single green banknote lying flat, with a short black dash line
under it going nowhere.

**SCENE 5 — 0:16–0:21 · MAKE IT CONCRETE**
**VO:** "Picture a thousand dollars sitting in your account for ten years."
**DRAW:** A green banknote labeled "$1,000" inside a simple bank building outline with two columns
and a triangular roof. A small black clock face beside it with "10 yrs" written under it.

**SCENE 6 — 0:21–0:26 · THE STING**
**VO:** "It's still a thousand dollars. It just doesn't buy a thousand dollars of anything anymore."
**DRAW:** The same banknote, but visibly shrunken to half size. Beside it, a shopping cart drawn
half-empty. A dashed black arrow from the old note outline to the smaller one.

**SCENE 7 — 0:26–0:31 · THE CONTRAST**
**VO:** "Meanwhile, money that's invested doesn't sit still. It compounds."
**DRAW:** New half of frame in blue: a small blue seedling, then an arrow to a taller plant, then an
arrow to a tree with gold coins hanging from its branches like fruit.

**SCENE 8 — 0:31–0:36 · THE REAL DIVIDE**
**VO:** "That's the actual gap. Not income — direction. One pile drains, one pile grows."
**DRAW:** Two coin piles side by side. Left pile gold with a red arrow curving down and away from it.
Right pile blue with a black arrow curving up. Keep them the same starting height so the arrows do
the talking.

**SCENE 9 — 0:36–0:42 · REFRAME (hand the viewer control)**
**VO:** "You don't fix that with a bigger paycheck. You fix it by giving every dollar a job."
**DRAW:** The cartoon woman from Scene 1, now standing, handing a green banknote to a small drawn
character wearing a hard hat and holding a shovel. Write **"A JOB"** in bold red beside them.

**SCENE 10 — 0:42–0:48 · CTA**
**VO:** "Most people never learn this. Which one is your money doing right now?"
**DRAW:** Two empty checkboxes. Beside the first write "draining," beside the second write "growing."
Hover the marker tip over the boxes without ticking either — end the clip mid-hover on the open loop.

## 5. Production notes

- **Total word count:** ~135 words over 48 seconds ≈ 170 wpm. Unhurried, matching the reference's
  calm delivery. Don't rush it — the format's authority comes from the steady pace.
- **Red is a budget.** Three words only: `LEAK`, `A JOB`, and nothing else unless you cut one. The
  reference used red exactly once ("Truth") in ten seconds. Overusing it kills the emphasis.
- **Blue = the alternative path**, consistently, in every scene it appears. The reference used blue
  the same way (the second pointing hand, the diamond). Never let blue mean two things.
- **Never lift the hand between beats** — draw continuously and let the previous drawing stay in
  frame until the new one crowds it out, or wipe to clean white on the beat. Pick one and hold it.
- **Shoot it as one continuous overhead take** and speed-ramp the drawing in post to land each
  stroke on its voiceover line. That sync is the entire craft of the format.
- **The end frame is the retention trick.** Ending mid-hover on an unticked checkbox leaves the loop
  open, which is what drives the replay that Shorts rewards.
